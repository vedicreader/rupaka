board

The screen-guide board: a closed drawing language over a page, the check of each line, the page context and the stream of steps.

A learner looks at a page and says “I don’t get alpha bar”. A tutor answers on the page, as a teacher does at a whiteboard. It circles the term, draws an arrow to a label in the figure, moves the slider of the demo, fills a small worked example in a side panel, and speaks one short line for each step.

This module is the part of the board that knows nothing about learning:

  • the board language: a closed list of drawing commands, each tied to a thing on the page (an anchor), and the check of each line of the model’s reply;
  • the context: what the page shows, cleaned and cut to size;
  • the video cues: a quote from the transcript of a video, at its time;
  • the stream: each step goes to the page as soon as it is whole.

static/board.js draws a checked board on the page, and static/texparts.js gives a formula the ids of its parts. abhyasa and Leela both use this module and these files. The decisions about learning stay in abhyasa: the tutor tiers (tutor, then vault, then web), the trust rules of sources, and the prompts.

Import the helpers. The module needs only fastcore.

The files

A host serves the files of the board. assets gives their paths. bundle('js') is texparts.js and board.js as one script, and bundle('css') is the paper tokens and board.css as one style sheet. The pen libraries (Rough.js and perfect-freehand, MIT) are in the folder libs. The board loads them on its first use from the URL folder host.assets (default /vendor/), so a host serves libs there. The board’s side panel uses the hand font Patrick Hand: a host loads it with rupaka.render.font_css.

The script sets window.rupakaBoard and window.rupakaTex. It sets the old names window.abhyasaBoard and window.abhyasaTex to the same objects, so a host that uses the old names works.

The board language

A reply is JSON lines: one object on each line. The server checks each line alone, so a bad line drops and the rest draws. There are 17 commands. The model never writes HTML, CSS, script or a URL.

op fields what it does
step say, and src with quote starts a step; say is the spoken line. A quote is word for word from source src
circle, box, underline at a hand-drawn ring, box or line on the anchor
arrow from, to a curved arrow between two anchors
label at, text, side a short note beside the anchor
sketch at, pts a free line in the box of a figure anchor, to mark a part of the figure
point at moves a pointer mark to the anchor
clear n (optional) removes the marks of all steps, or of step n
demo_set set changes inputs of the step’s rupaka demo, only after its gate is open
video_at t, quote moves the explainer video to t seconds, and quotes its transcript there
panel title, head, cols, numbered, stages (each optional) starts the side panel at the top right of the window: a small title, a bold heading and the names of two columns. With stages, its three rows are a case, a picture and the symbols. A new panel starts a new beat
row cells, and to, at, chain (optional) adds one row to the side panel: the cells as boxes, then an arrow and to. With at, the page circles that thing and a hand-drawn curve links it to the row. With chain, the cells are words with arrows between them. A cell {"blank": value} hides its value until the next step
note text one quiet line in the side panel
node id, text one box of a flow diagram in the side panel. id is a short name (a letter, then letters, digits or _)
edge from, to, text (optional) an arrow from box from to box to of the flow diagram, with a short label. An arrow back to an earlier box is a loop
myth think, fact, why a card of a wrong idea: “You might think” think, “In fact” fact, and one line of why

Each stroke can have w: the word of say where it starts.

A sketch marks a part of a figure only, so no shape covers the words of the page. A picture of an idea goes in the side panel. For a formula, the tutor gives an example from day-to-day life and marks the page’s own formula.

The side panel is the place for a small concrete example (Explain). The tutor builds it one row in each step, from the real values on the page: its row names the anchor of the value in at. The page circles the value and draws a curve from it to the row, so the model writes no circle for it. A process of 3 to 5 steps is a panel with numbered rows, or one chain row. A pipeline, a loop or a branch is a flow diagram: boxes (node) and arrows between them (edge), one box or two in each step, so the diagram grows with the talk.

Three more shapes come from the research on teaching. A faded worked example solves its first row, and each later row hides one more part as a blank, for the learner to fill: the blank shows its value when the next step plays, or when the learner clicks it. A wrong idea card (myth) puts a common wrong idea next to the right one, with one line of why. A panel with stages goes from the concrete to the abstract in three rows: a case, a picture (a chain), then the symbols. A new panel line starts the next beat, for example from the values on the page to the big idea. The side panel is the one picture of Explain. Show is a rupaka demo.

The first line can be a header: {"op":"v","n":1,"sure":0.9,"need":"none"}. sure is how sure the model is, from 0 to 1. need says what it needs to be sure: none, vault or web.

Anchors

A stroke names a thing on the page. The page sends a list of anchors, each with an id, a kind and its text. A formula also has its tokens (the TeX of its parts), a figure has its labels (a box for each label in the image), and the demo has its inputs.

form example the place
an id "passage" the whole anchor
words {"a": "passage", "text": "closed form"} the words in the anchor
a maths token {"a": "m1", "tex": "\\bar\\alpha_t"} the part of the formula
a figure label {"a": "figure", "label": "a"} the label in the image
a box {"a": "figure", "box": [100, 200, 400, 600]} a part of the anchor, from 0 to 1000
a point {"a": "m1", "pt": [1100, 500]} a point beside the anchor, for the far end of an arrow

Two ways to write the same TeX must match. norm_tex takes out the spaces and the braces around one symbol.

The check of one line

check_line gives the reason that a line drops, or ''. Each string in a line is checked too: no tag, no script and no link can get in, also as the text of a label.

A cue of the explainer video is [t, text]. cue_at gives the cue that plays at t seconds. cue_quote finds a quote in the cue at t or in the cue before or after it. It gives the start of the cue where the quote begins, and the quote as the transcript says it. The board seeks the video to that start, so a time that the model gives a little late still lands on the right cue.

Repair a panel line

A model often writes a panel line that is a little too long: an arrow label of a full sentence, a box with a second line, a note with a second sentence. When such a line drops, the picture breaks: a flow loses an arrow, and a panel loses its title. So repair_line makes the line good when one part of it is bad, and the rest of the line stays:

  • A panel line loses a bad title, head or column names.
  • An arrow loses a long label. The arrow is the structure of the flow; the label only names it.
  • A box keeps the first line of its text, or the name before a colon (Weight: a number to adjust is Weight).
  • A long note keeps its first whole sentences that fit. The maths markup does not count, only what shows.

The whole reply

parse_board reads a reply line by line. Each step keeps at most 6 strokes, and a board has at most 6 steps. A stroke before the first step drops. Two dropped lines in a row end the board (failed): the steps before them stay. A dropped line of the side panel does not count for this: it is a long label or a long text, not a reply that went wrong.

A step can quote a source: src is the number of the source in ctx.sources, and quote must be word for word in its text, with 40 to 600 characters. verbatim checks it: white space is made single, and the quote goes from a word boundary to a word boundary. A host can give its own check as verbatim(quote, text) -> str (abhyasa gives the rules of its sources). A quote that fails drops, and the step stays, in the tutor’s words.

Each row with its step

A step says one thing, and its row and its marks show that same thing. So the spoken line of a step must name what its row shows. key_terms gives the key words and symbols of a text: each word of 3 letters or more that is not a small word, each number, and each maths token such as x_i. named is true when a row (or a note) and a spoken line share one of them. The check is cheap and knows no subject. page_terms gives the key terms of what the page shows.

The panel is an example. It can come from the page, or it can be the tutor’s own case (“deva + ālaya → devālaya” on a page that names no word). So parse_board drops a row that names no word of the page only when another row of its panel does, and when it shares no word with the other rows or the title and head of its panel: then it is the odd one out, such as a later idea at the end of a worked example. A row with only numbers or symbols, and a box of a flow diagram, never drop for this: a diagram with one box less is broken. Then it pairs each row, note and box with its step (_pair). A row that its own step does not name moves to the next step of its panel that names it. A row that no step names drops. A row never moves back to an earlier step, so a stream sends each row once, with the step that explains it.

One shape for each panel

A panel is a table, a list or a flow diagram. Its lines line up only when they have one shape. A row is a pair (one or more cells, then an arrow and the answer to) or a chain (words with an arrow between each two). _shape sets the shape:

  • A flow diagram: the first line of the panel is a box (node). Its boxes and arrows (edge) stay, and rows drop. An arrow goes right after the later of its two boxes (_edges), and an arrow to a box that is not there drops.
  • A table: the panel has column names cols, and its first two rows are pairs with one cell less than the names and an answer. The names stand over the cells and the answers, so a later row that does not fit them drops. A chain of two words fits as one cell and its answer.
  • A list: else the names go and every row stays. A list lines up its chains on one grid, and the answer of a pair goes in the last column. Two short names say less than the rows, so the rows win.
  • A card of wrong ideas: the first line is a myth. It takes at most 2 of them, and no rows or boxes.
  • Three stages: a panel with stages is a list of at most 3 rows: a case, a picture, then the symbols. It has no column names.
  • A faded worked example: a row can hide parts as blanks. The first row of a panel shows its blanks, because it is the worked one.
  • At most 6 rows, 6 boxes and 8 arrows. More do not fit next to the page.

The first two rows decide, and not all of them, because a stream sends each row with its step: a later row cannot change the rows that show. So a stream holds a panel with column names until its second row is in (_held).

The context

The page sends what it shows: its address, the step, the concept, the anchors, the demo, the video, and what the learner showed with the pointer (the focus), and the thread: the last questions on this page. clean_context keeps only the keys that it knows and cuts the text: at most 40 anchors, 300 characters an anchor and 6000 in all. An anchor id has only letters, digits and :_-. An unknown kind becomes text. A try anchor is the last graded try of the open check (see The learner’s answer): it keeps its own fields, each with its own bound (TRY). The result is a PageContext. A host cleans each context that is not one (abhyasa’s explain does), so a raw dict is cleaned too, even when it has a by key.

What the learner shows

While the learner talks or types the question, they can circle a part with the mouse, or hold the pointer still on it. The host finds the gestures and sends them as focus: a list of items, the latest first. Each item has a kind (circle or point), the anchors under it (the best one first), a box from 0 to 1000 of its first anchor, and t, the milliseconds from the start of the question. An item can have words: what the learner said or typed near that time.

_focus keeps at most 5 good items. It keeps only the anchor ids that the page sent, and drops an item with none of them, with a bad kind or with a bad box.

The thread of a page

A learner who asks “say that more simply” or “why?” after a board means that board. So the page keeps a short thread in memory, and sends it with the next question: the last 3 turns on this page, each with the question (said), the lines that its board said (says) and its tier (t). The page forgets the thread when the page changes, after 10 minutes with no question, or when the learner pushes Start fresh. The server keeps no thread: each question brings its own.

_thread keeps the good turns, the oldest first: a question of at most 300 characters, at most 6 lines of at most 160 characters, and a known tier. A turn with no question or no line drops, and so does a turn with a tag or a link in a line, which a board never says. At most 1500 characters stay in all: the oldest turn goes first.

The demo part keeps the inputs (with their value, range and label), the readouts, and whether the gate is open.

The board as it comes

A board takes some seconds to write. So the host streams it, and the first step draws while the model writes the next one. stream_board reads the reply as it comes. When a new line is in, it reads the whole lines so far again with parse_board, so the rules are the same as for a whole reply. When the header says sure of SURE or more, on_step(step, k) gets each step as soon as its spoken line and its first mark are in, and again each time it gets more strokes: so the first mark does not wait for the rest of the step (the rows of a side panel) or for the next step. The page adds the strokes that it does not have yet. A step that is not sure, or a reply with no header, sends nothing: the host can ask again with sources (abhyasa looks in the vault, then on the web). The board that stream_board returns is the truth: its first steps are the steps that went to on_step.

When the stream breaks after a step went out, the board keeps the whole lines that came, so the page does not lose what it shows.

The learner can stop: a newer question, Esc, or a closed tab. Then the host sets the threading.Event stop. stream_board reads no more chunks and closes the stream. So the model call ends at once, and the next question does not wait for the backend.
